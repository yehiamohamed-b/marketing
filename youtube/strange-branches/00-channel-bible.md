# Strange Branches — Channel Bible

> **One-line positioning:** Cinematic documentaries about the branches life *didn't* take — and the ones it still might.

Every session (human or AI) working on this channel reads this file first.

---

## 1. Concept

Strange Branches is a faceless, long-form speculative documentary channel. Each episode takes one "what if" and follows it with scientific discipline across deep time — evolution, planets, alien ecosystems, and even gods and abstract concepts treated as if they were living things.

The **tree of life** is the core metaphor: every video is a branch. Some branches are real-but-forgotten, some are alternate, some are future, some are impossible.

### What makes us different
| Most "what if" channels | Strange Branches |
|---|---|
| Disaster spectacle, 5-second facts | Deep-time storytelling: millions of years, one coherent ecosystem |
| Generic AI visuals | A consistent "naturalist's field journal meets nature doc" look |
| No world rules | Every creature obeys real biology (physics, energy, ecology) — explained on screen |
| One-off topics | Recurring **worlds** and **creatures** viewers follow across episodes |
| Avoids myth | **Gods & Concepts** pillar — mythology and ideas analysed as evolving organisms (no one else does this) |

## 2. Audience

**Primary:** 18–34, mostly male, curious-nerd. Fans of *All Tomorrows*, *After Man*, *The Future Is Wild*, Kurzgesagt, Melodysheep, Sandbox Mode, CreatureArchives; worldbuilders, D&D/fantasy writers, sci-fi readers.

**Secondary:** Worldbuilders and writers looking for creature/ecosystem inspiration (proven by Sawyer Lee's "Make Your Fantasy Races Weird with Speculative Evolution" — 81K views).

**What they want:** awe + plausibility. "That's horrifying… but it makes sense."

**Viewing context:** Long sessions, often at night, headphones, second-screen or falling-asleep listens → favours 12–25 min runtimes, calm narration, ambient score.

## 3. Content pillars

| # | Pillar | Share | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Future Evolution** — what life becomes after us / after extinctions | 35% | "What Will Evolve After Humans Are Gone?" |
| 2 | **Alternate Earths** — planetary-scale what-ifs with evolutionary consequences | 30% | "What If the Dinosaurs Never Died?" |
| 3 | **Alien Biology** — full ecosystems on real or plausible exoplanets | 20% | "Life on a Tidally Locked Planet" |
| 4 | **Gods & Concepts** — myths, gods and ideas as organisms that evolve, compete and go extinct | 15% | "If Gods Evolved Like Animals" |

Pillar 4 is the brand's signature. Keep it rarer so it stays special, and always ground it in real anthropology / religious history / memetics.

## 4. Voice & tone

> **UPDATED:** the channel now uses the Curious Archive–style thought-experiment format and a witty, curious host voice. See `05-format-playbook.md` §5, which overrides "The Archivist" below. The rules on real-science anchors, time stamps and named creatures still apply.

- **Narrator persona:** "The Archivist" — a calm, slightly ominous naturalist cataloguing the strange branches of possibility. Never seen. Speaks like David Attenborough reading a forbidden field journal.
- **Tone:** wonder > fear. Dark is fine; gratuitous gore is not.
- **Rules:**
  1. Open with a concrete image, never "Have you ever wondered…".
  2. Every speculative claim gets a real-science anchor ("This isn't fantasy — on Earth, the anglerfish already…").
  3. Time stamps on screen: `+10,000 YEARS`, `+5 MILLION YEARS`.
  4. Name every creature (Latin-ish binomial + common name). Names = merch, community, recurring characters.
  5. End on a philosophical turn that seeds the next video.
- **Sign-off line:** *"Every branch is real somewhere. This has been Strange Branches."*

## 5. Visual identity

> **UPDATED:** most footage now comes from gameplay capture, simulations, short film/TV clips, stock and CC paleoart. AI is used only for creatures that don't exist (~10–20% of runtime). See `05-format-playbook.md` §3. The look below applies to AI creature art, thumbnails and graphics.

- **Look:** painterly-realistic "speculative naturalist" art — think a museum diorama meets a field-journal illustration, rendered cinematically.
- **Palette:** deep teal / bioluminescent cyan / bone white / amber accents. Dark backgrounds.
- **Recurring elements:**
  - Branching-tree timeline graphic that grows each episode (the "Branch Map").
  - Field-journal specimen cards (name, size, diet, ancestor, era) — great Shorts and community posts.
  - Time-jump transitions: tree rings / strata layers.
- **Thumbnail formula:** ONE strange creature or planet, close-up, eye contact if it has eyes, high contrast against dark background, ≤4 words of text (often none), one cyan or amber accent. Examples: *"AFTER HUMANS"*, *"66M YEARS LATER"*.

## 6. Format spec (long-form)

| Element | Spec |
|---|---|
| Length | 15–22 min (pilot ~18 min); test 30–60 min compilations from month 3 |
| Cadence | 1 long-form/week (Months 1–3), aim for 2/week once pipeline is stable |
| Shorts | 3–5 per long-form, cut from it (creature reveals, specimen cards) |
| Structure | Cold open → The rules → Problem 1…6 → Brief rant → Experiment → Completed world → Big-idea close → CTA (see `05-format-playbook.md` §2) |
| Retention devices | Time counters, "but something was about to change" turns, creature reveals every ~90 sec |

## 7. Originality & policy guardrails

YouTube demonetises "inauthentic / mass-produced" content. To stay monetisable with an AI-assisted pipeline:
- Every script is researched, human-edited and has a distinct POV (world rules, named creatures, recurring lore).
- Use a consistent narrator voice and style — the channel should feel *authored*, not generated.
- Disclose realistic synthetic content when YouTube's altered-content setting requires it (photoreal footage of things that could be mistaken for real).
- Never reuse another channel's footage or copy *All Tomorrows* / *After Man* creature designs — reference them, then build our own.
