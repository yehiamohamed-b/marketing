## COLD OPEN (0:00–0:50)

`[GAME: Bloodborne]` The camera pans up to Amygdala hanging off the Grand Cathedral.
`[GFX]` A record scratch; the frame freezes.

Every cosmic horror story has lied to you about one thing.

It isn't the tentacles, the eyes, or the "unknowable geometry" (whatever that means).

It's lunch.

`[GAME: Elden Ring]` Astel, Naturalborn of the Void, floating in the dark.

Because something this big has to *eat*, and *breathe*, and *think*, and somehow not collapse under its own weight like a soufflé that's been told bad news.

`[FILM: The Thing (1982)]` The kennel transformation (2 s).

So today we're going to answer the most irresponsible question in speculative biology:

**Could you build a real eldritch god without breaking a single law of biology?**

No magic. No "it's from another dimension, don't worry about it." Just evolution, physics, and a deeply unqualified narrator.

`[GFX]` **STRANGE BRANCHES** title sting.

## ACT 0: MEET THE CAST (0:50–2:30)

`[GFX]` A "cast introduction" card, styled like the opening credits of a heist movie. Freeze frame and name tag for each.

Every great disaster begins with a lovable team.

**First, me.** `[GFX]` Name tag: "THE HOST. Qualifications: 850 hours in Elden Ring."

I have spent eight hundred and fifty hours in Elden Ring. I have spent more time in the Lands Between than I have in the actual dentist's chair, and I've been told that this qualifies me for absolutely nothing. I'm here anyway.

**Second, the Lab.** `[SIM]` The Evolution Lab, creatures hopping around in 3D. Name tag: "THE LAB. A petri dish with anxiety."

This is our evolution simulator. It is very good at one thing: showing us what happens when nature makes a decision we didn't ask for.

**And third, our subject.** `[STOCK]` A sea slug (a nudibranch) gliding across a rock. Name tag: "GERALD. 3 centimetres. Future god."

This is Gerald. Gerald is a sea slug. Gerald has never hurt anyone. Gerald eats algae and minds his own business.

And by the end of this video, Gerald will be a Great Old One.

`[GFX]` The mission card is stamped in red: **OPERATION: PROMOTE GERALD.**

The rules are simple: every step has to be something real biology can do, or has already done, somewhere on Earth. If biology says no, we have to find a workaround.

Easy. What could possibly go wrong?

## ACT 1: MAKE IT BIG (2:30–5:00)

`[GFX]` Chapter card: **PROBLEM 1: SIZE**
`[GAME: Dark Souls 3]` Yhorm the Giant looming. `[GAME: Elden Ring]` Godfrey and the Fell Omen silhouettes; a Giant in the Mountaintops.

Step one is obvious: eldritch gods are *big*. Cthulhu is described as a "mountain [that] walked or stumbled." So we take Gerald and we scale him up a thousand times.

`[AI]` A kilometre-tall Gerald stands up… and immediately folds like a beach chair.

And Gerald dies instantly.

`[GFX]` A cube that doubles in size. Its surface ×4, its volume ×8.

This is the **square-cube law**, and Galileo figured it out in 1638. When you double an animal's size, its weight goes up eight times, but the strength of its bones and muscles only goes up about four times. Keep scaling, and eventually the body can't hold itself up.

`[ART]` Argentinosaurus skeleton next to a human, credited.

It's why the biggest land animals ever, the sauropod dinosaurs, topped out around seventy tonnes, and they needed hollow, air-filled bones and legs like Greek columns to do it.

`[GAME: Elden Ring]` A Giant in the Mountaintops. `[GFX]` Red "STRUCTURALLY IMPOSSIBLE" stamp.

So every towering giant in every game you love is, biologically speaking, a very large pile of broken legs waiting to happen. Sorry, Yhorm.

### The fix: go to the ocean

`[FILM: Pacific Rim]` A kaiju rising from the sea (2 s). `[GAME: Subnautica]` A Reaper Leviathan roar. `[BOOK]` *The Call of Cthulhu*: R'lyeh sleeping beneath the waves.

There's a reason every great cosmic monster lives underwater: Cthulhu in R'lyeh, the Leviathan, Subnautica's Ghost Leviathans. Water holds you up.

`[STOCK]` A blue whale gliding past.

The blue whale is up to thirty metres long and can weigh well over a hundred and fifty tonnes, which makes it bigger than any dinosaur we know of. It only works because the ocean carries its weight.

So: Gerald goes to sea. Gerald grows. Gerald becomes the size of three blue whales, then ten, then a submarine fleet.

`[GFX]` Mission tracker: **SIZE ✓**

Problem solved.

**BUT THEN…** `[GFX]` the foreshadowing stamp.

## ACT 2: FEED IT (5:00–7:45)

`[GFX]` Chapter card: **PROBLEM 2: LUNCH**
`[AI]` Giant Gerald drifting in the open ocean, its stomach growling and the bubbles visible.

…Gerald is starving.

`[STOCK]` A blue whale lunge-feeding on krill.

A 2021 study found that a blue whale can eat around **sixteen tonnes** of krill a day during feeding season. That's one animal. Now scale it up to a city-sized god, and you would need to strip-mine the ocean every week just to keep it awake.

`[GAME: Warhammer 40,000: Dawn of War / Space Marine 2]` A Tyranid swarm. `[GFX]` "This is basically the Tyranid business plan."

The only cosmic monsters in fiction that actually take this seriously are the Tyranids from Warhammer 40,000. They don't eat planets for fun; they eat planets because they have a *biomass budget*. They consume everything, then reprocess it in digestion pools. Honestly, it's the most biologically responsible thing in the entire grimdark future, and that's a very sad sentence.

`[VOX]` A friend: "So the scariest monster is just… very hungry?" / Me: "Have you ever met a teenager?"

### The fix: stop being one animal

`[STOCK]` A siphonophore: a glowing, string-like colony in the deep sea.

Nature has a cheat code for this, and it's terrifying. Meet the **siphonophore**: a creature that isn't a creature. It's a colony of thousands of tiny, specialised clones. Some only swim, some only sting, some only digest, and they're fused into one body. In 2020, researchers off Western Australia filmed a giant spiralling siphonophore estimated at roughly forty-five metres long. That's longer than a blue whale, built out of tiny parts that each feed themselves.

`[STOCK/ART]` Honey fungus spreading through a forest floor.

And on land, the largest known organism on Earth isn't an animal at all. It's a single honey fungus in Oregon's Blue Mountains, *Armillaria ostoyae*. It spreads underground across almost ten square kilometres and is estimated to be thousands of years old. Mostly invisible, and slowly eating a forest.

`[GAME: Halo 3]` The Gravemind. `[GAME: StarCraft]` Zerg creep. `[GAME: Bloodborne]` the Kos parasite.

So we rebuild Gerald. Not one body, but a colony, a network, a living city that feeds itself in a thousand places at once. Halo's Gravemind, the Zerg, the Flood. It turns out the sci-fi writers were onto something.

`[GFX]` Mission tracker: **SIZE ✓ LUNCH ✓**

**BUT THEN…** `[GFX]` stamp.

## ACT 3: MAKE IT SMART (7:45–11:00)

`[GFX]` Chapter card: **PROBLEM 3: GERALD CAN'T THINK**
`[AI]` Colony-Gerald, vast and glowing… being harpooned by a tiny boat. A beat. Then "…ow," arriving several seconds late.

…Gerald is very, very slow.

`[GFX]` A nerve impulse travelling down a 1-kilometre line, with a stopwatch.

Your fastest nerves fire signals at roughly one hundred and twenty metres per second. That's fast for a human, but over a one-kilometre body, a signal takes more than **eight seconds** to arrive. A god that size could be stabbed, notice eight seconds later, and then take another eight seconds to decide how it feels about it.

`[GAME: Elden Ring]` A slow-motion Rennala, Queen of the Full Moon, being hit. `[GFX]` "Your god has been parried."

That's not an eldritch deity. That's a Souls boss with a two-business-day reaction time.

### The fix: don't have *a* brain

`[STOCK]` An octopus arm reaching on its own.

But again, Earth already solved this. An octopus has around **five hundred million neurons**, and about **two-thirds of them are in its arms**. Each arm can taste, decide and grab without asking the brain first. The octopus doesn't *have* a brain so much as it's a committee wearing a trench coat.

`[STOCK]` A time-lapse of yellow slime mould spreading toward oat flakes.

And it gets weirder. In 2010, Japanese researchers placed oat flakes in the pattern of the cities around Tokyo and let a slime mould, *Physarum polycephalum*, grow between them. The slime mould built a network almost as efficient as the actual Tokyo rail system, with no brain and no neurons. It's just one cell doing maths it shouldn't be able to do.

`[GAME: Armored Core VI]` A Coral surge and Ayre's voice line (short). `[GAME: Hollow Knight]` the Radiance.

This is exactly what Armored Core 6 gets right about Coral. It isn't one creature; it's a substance that networks, multiplies, *communicates*, and maybe thinks, spread across an entire planet. Vaatividya could explain the lore better than me; I'm the guy who was still learning to quick-boost at the time.

`[BOOK]` *Solaris* (Stanisław Lem) cover. `[BOOK]` *Blindsight* (Peter Watts) cover.

Science fiction has gone even further. In *Solaris*, a whole ocean is a single mind that humans spend a century failing to understand. And in Peter Watts' *Blindsight*, the aliens are brilliant but have no inner experience at all. They're intelligence without anyone home.

So Colony-Gerald thinks the way a slime mould thinks: everywhere at once, slowly, and with nobody in charge.

`[GFX]` Mission tracker: **SIZE ✓ LUNCH ✓ BRAIN ✓**

### BRIEF RANT

`[GFX]` "RANT MODE" card.

*"Hey, isn't Gerald now just a big fungus?"*

…Yes. Our god is technically mould. But listen: in Elden Ring, one of the most feared Outer Gods is literally the **God of Rot**. Malenia's whole deal is a divine decomposer. Warhammer has Nurgle, the plague grandfather who *loves you*. Mould is a perfectly respectable career path for a deity.

**BUT THEN…** `[GFX]` stamp.

## ACT 4: THE MADNESS PROBLEM (11:00–14:30)

`[GFX]` Chapter card: **PROBLEM 4: WHY DOES IT DRIVE YOU INSANE?**
`[GAME: Bloodborne]` Insight going up, the screen's frenzy meter filling. `[GAME: Alan Wake 2]` the Dark Place. `[GAME: Dead Space]` Isaac hallucinating Nicole.

…because a god this big has a PR problem: nobody would be scared of it. It's a giant mould in the ocean. You can't even see it.

So we hit the last rule of cosmic horror: **seeing it makes you lose your mind.** In Bloodborne, gaining Insight lets you see the Great Ones that were always there. In Alan Wake, the Dark Presence rewrites the people around Cauldron Lake. In Dead Space, the Markers make people hallucinate, build more Markers, and then become Necromorphs.

Can biology make *madness* contagious?

Unfortunately… yes.

### Clue 1: brains can be hacked by shapes

`[STOCK]` An oystercatcher trying to sit on a giant fake egg.

In the 1950s, biologist Niko Tinbergen found that birds would ignore their own eggs to sit on a giant, fake, oversized one. Brains are built with shortcuts, and an exaggerated version of the right signal, a **supernormal stimulus**, overrides everything else.

`[STOCK]` An Australian jewel beetle on a brown beer bottle.

My favourite example: in Australia, male jewel beetles were found trying to mate with discarded brown beer bottles, because the bottles looked like the most attractive female beetle in history. It won an Ig Nobel Prize. This is a real thing that happened.

So a predator that evolved to look like the most important shape a human brain can see (eyes, faces, *patterns*) could jam our perception like a beer bottle jams a beetle. `[GAME: Bloodborne]` Amygdala's eyes. That's not magic, that's advertising.

### Clue 2: sound you can't hear

`[GFX]` A waveform at 18.9 Hz below the hearing threshold.

In 1998, engineer Vic Tandy was working in a lab that staff believed was haunted. He kept seeing a grey shape in the corner of his eye, and he felt dread. Then he found that a newly installed extractor fan was producing a sound at about **19 hertz**, below human hearing, which may vibrate the eyeball and trigger anxiety. When the fan was turned off, the "ghost" vanished.

`[STOCK]` Whale song. `[GFX]` "The Bloop" audio waveform.

A city-sized god breathing in the deep ocean would produce *enormous* low-frequency sound. (Remember "the Bloop," that mysterious ocean noise from 1997 that people swore was a giant creature? It turned out to be an icequake. But you get the idea.)

### Clue 3: parasites that rewrite behaviour

`[STOCK]` An ant infected with *Ophiocordyceps*, clamped to a leaf. `[GAME: The Last of Us]` a Clicker.

And here's the worst one. The fungus *Ophiocordyceps* infects ants, takes over their behaviour, makes them climb to the perfect height, bite down on a leaf and die, and then grows out of their heads to rain spores on the colony below. The Last of Us is based on this.

`[STOCK]` A rat calmly approaching cat scent.

*Toxoplasma gondii*, a single-celled parasite, makes infected rats *lose their fear of cats*, which delivers the parasite back into cats, where it reproduces. Researchers estimate that roughly a quarter to a third of all humans carry it.

`[GAME: Sekiro]` the Senpou monks' centipedes; the Long-arm Centipede boss. `[GAME: Bloodborne]` the Kos parasite.

Sekiro got this right too: the monks' "immortality" turns out to be centipedes living inside their bodies. They aren't blessed; they're *infested*.

So here's the biological version of cosmic madness: Gerald doesn't need you to *see* him. Gerald needs you to *carry* him. The visions, the dread and the strange urge to build things near the ocean are just the parasite making its host spread its spores.

`[GAME: Dead Space]` Unitologists building a Marker.

Which, if you think about it, is exactly what a Marker is: a cult that does the infecting for it.

`[GFX]` Mission tracker: **SIZE ✓ LUNCH ✓ BRAIN ✓ MADNESS ✓**

**BUT THEN…** `[GFX]` stamp.

## ACT 5: FROM BEYOND THE STARS (14:30–17:00)

`[GFX]` Chapter card: **PROBLEM 5: WHERE DID GERALD EVEN COME FROM?**
`[GFX]` A geological timeline showing the fossil record, with no Gerald anywhere.

…we have a continuity error. If a god this big evolved on Earth, we'd have found its ancestors. Fossils, cousins, *something*. Cosmic horror always solves this the same way: *it came from the stars.*

`[GAME: Elden Ring]` the Greater Will sending the Elden Beast as a star. `[BOOK]` *The Colour Out of Space* (Lovecraft). `[TV: The Expanse]` the protomolecule. `[FILM: Prometheus]` the black goo.

Elden Ring's Greater Will sent the Elden Beast as a falling star. Lovecraft's *Colour Out of Space* arrives in a meteorite. The Expanse's protomolecule hitches a ride on an icy moon. Ridley Scott's Engineers seed worlds with black goo.

Can life actually survive a trip through space?

`[STOCK]` Tardigrades under a microscope.

In 2007, scientists strapped **tardigrades** (half-millimetre "water bears") to the outside of a satellite and exposed them to the vacuum of space. Many survived. The bacterium *Deinococcus radiodurans* can survive radiation doses thousands of times higher than what would kill a human. The idea that life could travel between worlds inside rocks is called **panspermia**, and it's a real, if unproven, scientific hypothesis.

`[GAME: Warhammer 40,000]` A Tyranid hive fleet emerging from the intergalactic dark.

So this is Gerald's real origin story. He's not a giant. He's a *spore*, tiny, dormant and indestructible, drifting between stars like a Tyranid hive fleet crossing the void between galaxies.

`[GFX]` Mission tracker: **SIZE ✓ LUNCH ✓ BRAIN ✓ MADNESS ✓ ORIGIN ✓**

**BUT THEN…** `[GFX]` the stamp, bigger this time.

## ACT 6: THE EXPERIMENT (17:00–19:00)

`[GFX]` Chapter card: **PROBLEM 6: WHAT HAPPENS WHEN IT LANDS?**
`[SIM]` Evolution Lab, *Earth as usual* preset, running calmly.

Gerald lands, and we don't have to guess what happens next, because we can run it.

Here's a calm, ordinary world in our Evolution Lab: grazers, a few omnivores, everybody minding their own business, like Gerald used to.

`[SIM]` Press **Asteroid**; the impact; the "radiation rising" toast. Switch on **Sense rings**, and let it run at ×10.

So I drop a spore-carrying impact into the middle of it. The blast clears out a chunk of the world, and the "radiation", our stand-in for a new alien invader rewriting life's code, sends mutation through the roof.

> **[RECORD THIS SEGMENT, THEN WRITE THE RESULT. Narration template:]**
> "Watch what happens over the next few thousand generations. The survivors ______. The average size ______, and hunting ______. It's not that the asteroid *built* monsters. It shook the world hard enough that evolution started rolling new dice, and the dice it kept were ______."

`[TV: Annihilation (2018 film)]` the Shimmer's refracted flowers (short). `[GAME: Dead Space 3]` a Brethren Moon.

That's exactly the nightmare in *Annihilation*: the Shimmer doesn't attack anyone; it just *mixes* everything's DNA until the world isn't the world any more. And if you keep scaling that up, you get Dead Space 3's Brethren Moons: a "god" that isn't living on a planet. It *is* the planet.

`[AI]` Final reveal: an alien world from orbit, its oceans laced with glowing fungal networks in the shape of a vast, sleeping face.

So here is our finished, biologically accurate eldritch god:

- It's not one creature. It's a **colony**, like a siphonophore.
- It doesn't think with a brain. It thinks with a **network**, like a slime mould.
- It doesn't hunt you. It **infects** you, like Cordyceps.
- It doesn't drive you mad with magic. It uses **supernormal stimuli and infrasound**.
- It wasn't born here. It drifted in as a **spore**, like a tardigrade in a rock.
- And it doesn't live *on* the world. Eventually, it **is** the world.

`[GFX]` Mission tracker: everything ticked. The stamp reads **OPERATION: PROMOTE GERALD — COMPLETE.**

Congratulations, Gerald. You're a god now.

## ACT 7: THE FINAL "BUT THEN" (19:00–20:45)

`[GFX]` A slow push-in on the mission board. The music drops out.

But then I realised something, and I really didn't enjoy it.

`[STOCK]` Honey fungus under a forest. `[STOCK]` A siphonophore. `[STOCK]` A Cordyceps ant. `[STOCK]` A slime mould.

To build our eldritch god, I didn't have to invent a single new ability. Every part of it already exists, right now, on Earth. There's a fungus the size of a town, a colony longer than a whale, a cell that solves rail networks, a parasite that rewrites behaviour (one that may be living in roughly a quarter of the people watching this), and animals that survive the vacuum of space.

The only thing we added was *putting them all in one place*.

`[BOOK]` H.P. Lovecraft, *Supernatural Horror in Literature* (1927), quote on screen.

Lovecraft wrote that "the oldest and strongest emotion of mankind is fear, and the oldest and strongest kind of fear is fear of the unknown."

But I think biology flips that. The scariest thing isn't the unknown. It's realising how much of the unknown is already *known*, and it's just very small, very patient and growing in the dirt under your feet.

`[GAME: Bloodborne]` Micolash: "Grant us eyes…" (short).

Bloodborne's scholars begged the cosmos to "grant us eyes" so they could see the Great Ones. Honestly? All you need is a microscope.

## Why this script works (structure analysis)
| Device | From Curious Archive | How we made it ours |
|---|---|---|
| Hook question to the viewer | "What's the scariest thing in the Bible?" | "Every cosmic horror has lied to you about… lunch." A joke and a promise in one line |
| Escalating ladder | Angel hierarchy, getting stranger each rank | Each act unlocks a "god trait" (size → lunch → brain → madness → origin → takeover) |
| Open loops / callbacks | "Hold on to that question…" | The "BUT THEN" stamp and a mission tracker that fills up on screen |
| Vox pops | Interviews with other creators | `[VOX]` slots: record 20–30 s answers from friends, a Discord or co-op group ("What scares you most about Cthulhu?") |
| Pop-culture anchors | Pacific Rim, Smaug, LotR | Elden Ring, Bloodborne, Sekiro, DS3, AC6, Warhammer, Dead Space, Alien, Alan Wake, Halo, Subnautica |
| Scholarly anchor | Esther Hamori, *God's Monsters* | Real studies: blue whale feeding, Tokyo slime mould, tardigrades in space, Tinbergen, Vic Tandy, Toxoplasma |
| Big-idea close | "It's us who abandoned them" | "The unknown is already known. It's just small." |
| Self-deprecation | "Most advanced tech on this channel" | 850-hours-in-Elden-Ring host, "deeply unqualified narrator", the Vaati joke |

**The "Obsession" escalation, adapted:** *funny cast intro → mission → problem → fix → BUT THEN → bigger problem → fix → BUT THEN…* Each "BUT THEN" is a **biological** obstacle, never a random plot twist, so the comedy structure keeps teaching. The last "BUT THEN" turns on the audience (*it's already here*) instead of on the plot.
