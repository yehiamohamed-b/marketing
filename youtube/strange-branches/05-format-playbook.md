# Format Playbook: The "Curious Archive" Model, Adapted for Strange Branches

Reference: Curious Archive, "An Ecosystem of Predators" (https://youtu.be/nTHhDsFbRYo).
Goal: understand **why** that format works, and how to build Strange Branches videos the same way without copying them.

---

## 1. Why their videos feel more interesting than a pure spec-evo documentary

| What they do | Why it works | What it means for us |
|---|---|---|
| **One absurd premise, taken seriously** ("what if cows were predators?") | The premise is the hook; the rigour is the payoff | Every title is a single, instantly-graspable "what if" |
| **Problem-by-problem structure** (Energy → Herbivores → Apex predators → Humans → Cooperation) | Each chapter is a mini-mystery with its own answer. Viewers keep watching to see the next "problem" solved | Script = a list of 5–7 *problems* the premise creates, each solved with real science |
| **A host with personality** (jokes, asides, fake arguments with himself, "brief rant") | It feels like a smart friend thinking out loud, not a lecture | Narrator is a curious, witty host. Faceless, but with a voice and opinions |
| **Real-world anchors everywhere** (horses eat chicks, dragonfly 95% hunt rate, buffalo formations) | Every "wait, really?" fact earns trust and gets shared | 1 surprising real fact every 60–90 seconds |
| **Cheap but clever "experiments"** (MinuteLabs blob sim, modded Minecraft) | Turns speculation into something *shown*, and it's funny | Each video has one EXPERIMENT segment: a sim or game we play to test the idea |
| **Footage from games, films, TV, docs + a little AI** | Fast to produce, visually rich, and recognisable pop culture adds humour | Our visuals come mostly from capture and clips, not generation (see §3) |
| **Pop-culture quotes and callbacks** (Dr. Henry Wu, Jurassic Park) | Instant emotional shorthand | Close each video with a quote or callback that reframes the premise |
| **Ends on a bigger idea and a tease** | Rewards the viewer, sets up the next click | Philosophical close, a comment question, then the next-video tease |
| **Topic range is broad** (biology, worldbuilding, myth, space) | The *format* is the brand, not one topic | Keep all 4 pillars. **Kaelith** becomes one recurring series, not the whole channel |

## 2. The script skeleton (use for every episode)

| Section | Length | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Cold open** | 20–40s | 2–4 punchy lines: the normal world, the twist, the question stack. A joke within 15 seconds. |
| **The rules** | 30–60s | Define the thought experiment: what changes, what doesn't. |
| **Problem 1 … Problem 5–7** | 1.5–3 min each | Each opens with a question, explores a real anchor, hits an obstacle, then resolves or adjusts the rules. On-screen chapter card per problem. |
| **Brief rant / aside** | 30–60s | The host admits the limits ("there are problems I've glossed over…"). Builds trust and humour. |
| **The experiment** | 1.5–3 min | Simulation or game-mod segment. Shown, not told. |
| **The completed world** | 1.5–2 min | Tour of the resulting creatures or ecosystem. This is where AI art or Kaelith-style designs appear. |
| **Big-idea close** | 30–60s | Reframe; quote; "maybe the real lesson is…" |
| **Outro / CTA** | 20–40s | Comment question, next-video tease, sub ask; sponsor or Patreon slot if any. |

**Target runtime:** 15–22 minutes (~2,400–3,300 words at ~150 wpm).

## 3. Visual sourcing (much less AI than Kaelith)

| Source | Use for | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Gameplay you capture yourself** (Jurassic World Evolution 2, Path of Titans, The Isle, ARK, Spore, Thrive, Species: ALRE, Equilinox, Planet Zoo, Minecraft + mods, Universe Sandbox) | 50–60% of b-roll | Lowest copyright risk. Most publishers allow monetised gameplay; check each game's video policy. Record in 4K, with no HUD where possible. |
| **Simulations** (MinuteLabs.io, Primer-style sims, Thrive, Species: ALRE, Universe Sandbox) | The EXPERIMENT segment | Credit the tool on screen. |
| **Film/TV/doc clips** (Jurassic Park, Prehistoric Planet, Walking with Dinosaurs, Planet Earth) | Short jokes, callbacks, real-animal footage | Fair use is about *transformative commentary*: short clips (≤5–10s), our voice over them, no long music beds. Expect occasional Content ID claims, and keep clips swappable. |
| **Stock** (Pexels, Pixabay, Storyblocks) | Real animals (horses, crows, buffalo) | Safe and cheap. |
| **Paleoart / museum images** (Wikimedia Commons CC-BY / public domain) | Real fossils and species | Always credit the artist and check the licence. |
| **AI images and video** (Midjourney / Magnific → Kling / Runway) | ONLY things that don't exist: our speculative creatures, Kaelith | ~10–20% of the runtime. Reuse the creature designs across episodes. |
| **Simple motion graphics** (CapCut / Canva) | Chapter cards, maps, food webs, timelines, jokes | Build one reusable template set. |

**Result:** a video needs maybe 10–25 AI shots instead of 100+. This is why the format is faster to make than Kaelith.

## 4. Where Kaelith fits
Kaelith (the speculative planet) becomes a **recurring series inside the channel**, like Curious Archive's worldbuilding deep-dives:
- **"Kaelith Files"**: one episode every 4–6 weeks, each exploring one problem of the planet (its gravity, its predators, its "plants").
- It's the one place where heavier AI rendering is worth it, because the assets get reused across the series.
- Main-channel episodes can cross-reference Kaelith ("on Kaelith, this problem is solved differently…") to funnel viewers into the series.

None of the Kaelith work is wasted. It becomes the channel's signature original world.

## 5. Voice guide (replaces "The Archivist" from the channel bible)
- **Persona:** curious, slightly chaotic, sincerely nerdy host. Faceless.
- **Devices:**
  - Parenthetical jokes: "(as far as we know)".
  - Self-interruptions and fake dialogue: *"Hey, are you going to finish this tangent?" "It's finished when I say it's finished."*
  - Grading or scoring jokes ("F-minus in not starving").
  - Lists of absurd examples ("cows, giraffes, rabbits, hippos — ").
  - Honesty beats: "This is pure conjecture… but that's kind of what we're here for."
- **Rules:** never mock the science; mock the premise. One real fact behind every joke.
- **Sign-off:** "Thanks for climbing out on this branch with me. See you on the next one."
